# Methodology

## Purpose

This checklist is a launch-readiness aid for public-facing political campaign websites. It focuses on whether a visitor can understand the candidacy, navigate the site, take an intended action, and use the site safely across common devices and access needs.

## Organization

Checks are grouped into eight review areas:

1. **Campaign identity** — who is running, for which office and district, and when the election occurs.
2. **Core content** — biography, issues, endorsements, events, news, and media.
3. **Actions and trust** — contact, volunteer, donation handoff, privacy, and paid-for/disclaimer review.
4. **Accessibility and mobile** — keyboard access, contrast, alternative text, labels, zoom, and small-screen behavior.
5. **Language quality** — complete language parity, human review, and reliable language switching.
6. **Technical readiness** — domain, HTTPS, redirects, error pages, performance, and broken-link testing.
7. **Search and measurement** — titles, descriptions, social previews, indexing controls, structured data, and consent-aware measurement.
8. **Operations** — ownership, backups, incident contacts, content cadence, and post-election transition.

## Severity

- **Critical:** a failure can prevent visitors from identifying the campaign, completing a core action, accessing the site, or understanding an important disclosure.
- **Recommended:** materially improves clarity, accessibility, discoverability, or operational resilience.

Severity is a triage aid, not a legal determination.

## Validation approach

Each item is written as an observable check. A reviewer should be able to mark it complete using one or more of:

- visual inspection on desktop and mobile
- keyboard-only navigation
- link and form testing
- browser developer tools
- search/social preview tools
- review by the campaign owner or a qualified adviser

## Boundaries

This project does not certify legal compliance, accessibility conformance, election-law compliance, security, or search ranking. Laws and platform behavior change, and requirements differ by jurisdiction and campaign. The checklist therefore asks teams to confirm review by appropriate professionals rather than asserting a universal legal rule.

## Maintenance

Suggestions should identify:

- the proposed check
- the failure it prevents
- how a campaign team can verify it
- whether it is critical or recommended
- a primary or authoritative source when the suggestion depends on a technical standard or law
